Who I Am

Dedicated to
human connection.

I am Sanskriti Nepal, a passionate, culturally grounded counseling psychology student working toward licensure as a Professional Counselor (LPC). My work in mental health is driven by an unwavering belief that every person deserves compassionate care that honors their unique story.

Pursuing my M.S. in Psychological Services, Counseling Psychology (MSPS) at East Central University, I am building a robust clinical foundation in therapeutic frameworks, psychological assessment, multicultural counseling, and crisis intervention.

Growing up in Nepal and studying in the United States, I bring a bicultural lens to everything I do, understanding how cultural identity, community belonging, and lived experience shape mental health.

Philosophy & Practice

The values
guiding my training.

I believe that meaningful change begins in a relationship built on trust, curiosity, and genuine respect for each person's lived experience.

"Mental health care is not a luxury; it is a fundamental human need. I believe that every person carries within them the capacity for growth and healing."

Empathetic Presence

I'm learning that meaningful therapeutic work begins with a client feeling genuinely heard, seen, and safe. I'm seeking to develop that capacity through supervised practice, coursework in counseling micro-skills, and ongoing self-reflection.

Cultural Humility

My bicultural background shapes how I approach every individual, with curiosity about their cultural context, identity, and worldview. I'm continuing to build this into a clinical skill through coursework in multicultural counseling and consultation.

Evidence-Based Practice

My coursework has grounded me in CBT, person-centered therapy, motivational interviewing, and trauma-informed approaches. I'm working toward integrating these thoughtfully as I move into supervised clinical practice.

Collaborative Process

I believe the people I work with are the experts on their own lives. As I move into clinical practice, I'm committed to working alongside them as a partner, centering their goals and values rather than imposing my own.

Reflective Growth

I'm committed to ongoing self-reflection and supervision, because the quality of care a future clinician offers is inseparable from the work they do on themselves.
